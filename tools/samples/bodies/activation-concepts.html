The brief

Show what "We're in business to save our home planet" looks like at a sold-out festival.

Audience

225,000 over three days in 2026, about 75,000 a day, sold out. The city asks them to come by bike, Muni or on foot.

Place

Eco Lands, the festival's environmental area since 2008, with a solar-powered stage and charging stations. Evenings in the park get cold and foggy.

Success looks like

Jackets repaired instead of replaced, clothing kept out of landfill, and Worn Wear trade-ins started on site.

Concept B · Stretch · Guest journey

The Worn Wear Fog Camp: bring the jacket you came in, and leave with it fixed.

  1. Arrive. A repair trailer and a warm, lantern-lit tent in Eco Lands, with a fog-level sign: "Too cold? Come in."
  2. Drop off. Hand over a torn jacket, broken zip or lost button. Any brand.
  3. Warm up. Wait in a heated lounge, with the main stage on a screen.
  4. Pick up. A text when it's ready, plus a patch stitched on if you want one.
  5. Leave with. Your own jacket, fixed. Worn-out pieces go to the festival's textile recycling.

Why it fits Patagonia

Repair over replace is the Worn Wear program, and the festival already has the recycling to finish the loop.

Throughput Estimate

About 720 repairs (6 stations × 4 an hour × 10 hours × 3 days)

Risks and fixes

  • Queue too long: cap drop-offs per hour and text people back.
  • Lost items: tagged tickets and photo at drop-off.
  • Heaters near fabric: radiant only, 3 ft clearance, fire marshal sign-off.

Measured by

  • Repairs: drop-off tickets
  • Landfill avoided: textile bins weighed
  • Trade-ins: Worn Wear sign-ups
